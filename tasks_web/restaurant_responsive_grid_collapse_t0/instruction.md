Most people read our menu standing in the lane on a phone, deciding whether the
queue is worth it — and on a phone the "The board tonight" section is a mess. The
six plate cards are jammed side by side into three slivers no matter how narrow
the screen gets, so "Fish-Fragrant Aubergine" wraps to one word per line, the
price pill collides with the dish name, and the description underneath turns into
a column of two-letter fragments. The four skewer tiles in the black panel below
are worse: four columns squeezed into a phone width, and "Chicken Heart" barely
fits.

On a phone the cards in that section should sit one under the other, each one
full width and comfortably readable, and only spread out into several columns
once there is real room for them — on a tablet and up. Every other card section
on the page already behaves this way if you narrow the browser window; this one
does not. On a laptop the section should look exactly as it does today, three
plates across with the skewers in a row of four underneath.

Nothing else should change: the same six plates with the same prices, heat
ratings and descriptions, the same four skewers, and every other section of the
page left alone.
