The "Everything a family needs, on one street" part of our site shows the six
treatments we offer as cards. On a laptop it looks right — three cards across,
two rows. On a phone it falls apart: the six cards are still crammed three
abreast into the width of the screen, so each one ends up about as wide as a
thumb. The prices wrap onto four lines, the little checklists under each
treatment turn into a column of single words, and "Children's dentistry" breaks
across three lines. More than half the people who visit us are on a phone, and
the front desk has had callers ask whether the price list is broken.

On a narrow screen the six treatments should simply run one under another, each
card getting the full width it needs to be readable. As the screen gets wider
they should open back out — a couple across on a tablet, and the three-across
layout we already have on a laptop. The other blocks on the page already behave
this way; this one does not.

Nothing else should change: the same six treatments in the same order, the same
prices, wording, icons and checklists, and the rest of the page exactly as it is.
