A guest wrote to us after trying to book a table with a screen reader, and then
we sat with her and went down the page ourselves. She could not get through it.

The room photographs came out as nothing at all. The large one under "Pick where
you want to sit" and the five small ones beside it were passed over in silence,
so she had no idea we had just shown her the hearth, the oyster counter, the
cutting shed, the harbour terrace and the cellar — which is the entire point of
that part of the page, and the thing the booking panel then asks her to choose
between.

The small round controls in the top corner are worse. Her reader called all four
of them just "button", one after another, so she had to press each one blind to
find out which gives the opening times, which gives directions and parking,
which is about gift cards and which rings the host stand. The two round arrow
controls next to the large photograph are the same — a pair of unnamed buttons,
with no way to tell which moves on to the next room and which goes back.

The reservation panel is where she gave up. She could hear "Full name", "Email
address", "Phone number", "Party size", "Seating area", "Anything we should
know" and the Friday board opt-in read out as she moved down the panel, but the
moment she landed in one of the boxes her reader announced nothing at all — so
she could not tell which of the seven she was actually typing into, and she left
without sending the request.

All of it should be announceable. Each room photograph should say which room it
is showing, including the large one, which should say whichever room is on
display at that moment. Each control that is only an icon should say what it
does. And each box in the reservation panel should be tied to the wording
already printed beside it, so that landing in a box announces the right thing.

This is about what assistive technology hears, not about how the page looks:
keep the same layout, copy, photographs, controls, options and fields exactly as
they are, and leave the rest of the page alone.
