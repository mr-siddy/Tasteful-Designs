Our site is one long page — the menu, the hearth, the room, the evening, the
cellar and the visit details all run one after another — and the bar of
destinations that stays stuck to the top is meant to tell you where in that page
you currently are, by lighting up whichever one you have reached.

It doesn't. "Menu" is lit when the page loads and it stays lit no matter how far
down you go: read the whole hearth piece, scroll through the room, the timings
and the entire cellar list, and the bar still says you are up at the menu.
Nothing else ever lights up, so on a page this long guests lose track of where
they are and use the browser scrollbar instead of the bar we built for them.

Whichever of those six parts of the page the guest is actually reading should be
the one marked in the bar — and only that one. The marking should move as they
scroll on down, and move back again if they scroll up. Someone using a screen
reader should be told the same thing the highlight tells everyone else, the way
the currently marked destination already announces itself today.

Leave the rest of the page as it is: the same six destinations in the same order,
the same sections, copy and prices, and the page still opening on the menu.
