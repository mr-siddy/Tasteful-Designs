An accessibility consultant walked our marketing page with a screen reader
before we shipped it to the Fernpost Bank procurement review, and she could not
get through it.

The product screenshots came out as nothing at all. The big one under "Every
release, on one screen" and the five smaller ones under it were skipped over in
silence, so she had no idea we had shown her the deploy timeline, the approval
queue, the evidence bundle, the blast-radius preview or the freeze calendar —
which is most of what that section is there to communicate.

The small round controls in the top right corner are worse. Her reader called
each of the four just "button", one after another, so she had to press all four
blind to work out which one searches the docs, which one shows what shipped in
the latest version, which one reports whether the control plane is up, and which
one is her account. The two round arrow controls beside the screenshots are the
same — a pair of unnamed buttons, and no way to tell which one moves forward and
which one moves back.

The signup panel is where she stopped. She could hear "Work email", "Full name",
"Company", "Services to start with", "Compliance regime", "What you want to
automate first" and the digest opt-in read out as she moved down the panel, but
the moment she landed in one of the boxes her reader announced nothing — so she
could not tell which of the seven she was actually typing into, and she gave up
before requesting a sandbox.

Everything on that page should be announceable. Each screenshot should say which
screen it is showing, including the large one, which should say whichever screen
is currently on display. Each control that is only an icon should say what it
does. And each box in the signup panel should be tied to the wording already
printed beside it, so landing in a box announces the right thing.

This is about what assistive technology hears, not about how the page looks:
keep the same layout, copy, screenshots, controls, options and fields exactly as
they are, and leave the rest of the page alone.
