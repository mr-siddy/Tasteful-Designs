# Brief — Copperline (saas · landing)

Build the marketing landing page for **Copperline**, field-service software sold
to residential and light-commercial trade contractors — the HVAC, plumbing,
electrical and refrigeration shops that run somewhere between one and forty
trucks. Copperline replaces the whiteboard, the group text and the shoebox of
parts receipts with a single live board: dispatchers drag a call onto a truck and
the tech's phone updates in seconds, every van carries a tracked parts bin that
decrements when a technician scans a part onto a job, quotes go out from the
driveway as good/better/best options the homeowner can accept on the spot, and
the invoice is issued and paid before the truck leaves the street. The company is
Copperline Software, Inc., 240 Ionia Avenue SW, Grand Rapids, Michigan; support
is staffed by former dispatchers 6am–9pm Eastern on (616) 555 0142; the flagship
line is **"Every truck, every part, every invoice — on one board."** The voice is
plain, unfussy and operational: this is sold to owners and dispatch leads who are
reading the page between calls, so the copy names real mechanics (dispatch-to-
accept time, truck stock, callback rate, maintenance agreements, aging
receivables) instead of adjectives. Named customers to reference throughout:
Whitlock Heating & Air (Kalamazoo, MI), Ferrer Plumbing Co. (Toledo, OH),
Northgate Electric (Fort Wayne, IN), Ridgeway Mechanical, Bell & Cutler HVAC and
Sawtooth Refrigeration.

Ship a long-form landing page on a warm cream ground (`#FBF6EE`) with a deep
slate-teal ink (`#12242C`), a copper accent (`#C25A2B`) and a service-green
signal (`#0E6A66`), all visuals drawn as inline SVG and CSS gradients because no
network is available. It needs: a site header with the brand mark, five
destinations, the support number and a walkthrough CTA; a hero carrying the
flagship line, a subhead of at least twenty words, two calls to action and a
drawn dispatch-board illustration; a trust bar with six contractor wordmarks and
four hard numbers; a six-card capability grid with drawn icons; three alternating
deep-dive blocks that actually explain the dispatch board, van stock and
invoicing; a four-trade fit section (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, refrigeration);
a four-step rollout path (import, map, shadow week, go live); **a three-plan
pricing section — the anchor section — with a monthly/annual billing toggle and
exactly one plan carrying the most-popular badge**: Solo at $79 for one truck,
**Crew at $189 for up to eight trucks — this is the promoted plan, the one the
badge belongs to**, and Fleet at $429 for unlimited trucks and branches; a
customer story from Whitlock Heating & Air with three real numbers and a quote;
three testimonials attributed to named people with real job titles at the named
shops; a six-question FAQ answering what an owner would actually ask about
switching software mid-season; a closing conversion block built around a short
labelled walkthrough form that confirms in place; and a four-column footer with
the address, the support hours and the legal row.

The pricing section is the page's commercial centre of gravity, so it has to be
unambiguous: Crew — the middle tier — is the plan Copperline promotes, it is the
only card that may carry the "Most popular" badge, and it keeps that badge
whether the visitor is looking at monthly or annual prices.
