# Brief — Ember & Rye (restaurant, landing)

Build the single-page marketing site for **Ember & Rye**, a nine-year-old wood-fire
supper house at 214 Halstead Row in the old Dunmore Mill district. Everything on the
menu meets live fire before it reaches a plate: one twelve-foot brick hearth, oak and
apple wood cut from an orchard forty minutes north, no gas line anywhere in the
kitchen. Chef-owner **Margo Ferrante** ran the pit at a barbecue counter for six years
before opening the room, and the personality of the page should match hers — plain,
confident, a little stubborn about sourcing, never precious. Palette: deep charcoal
(#171310-ish) and ember orange, warmed with a bone/cream neutral ramp and a smoke-grey
mid-tone; serif display type for headings against a clean sans for body; generous
section padding, soft-square radii, low warm shadows. Alternate section backgrounds
(bone → charcoal → cream) so the page has rhythm as a visitor scrolls. All visuals are
inline SVG — a hearth-arch spot illustration, a hand-drawn ember/rye brand mark, an
icon set for the trust bar and process steps, a dry-ager and sourcing-map diagram for
the deep-dive blocks, and gradient/mesh washes behind the hero and closing CTA. No
network is available, so no remote images.

The page runs long and flat: a sticky header with five destinations and a reserve
button; a hero with a real subhead; a trust bar of stats and regional awards; the
hearth menu (six dishes with prices and one honest sentence each); **the private-dining
packages section**; three alternating deep-dive blocks (the fire, the dry-ager, the
sixty-mile sourcing radius); a five-step booking process; the room and the four people
who run it; three named testimonials; six real FAQs; a visit block with hours, address
and a mailing-list form; a closing CTA; and a four-column footer.

The section the whole page is anchored on is **private dining packages**. Ember & Rye
books its back room three ways — **Hearthside** (up to 12 guests, the set family-style
board), **Chef's Table** (up to 24, six courses cooked at the hearth rail with Margo
working the fire in front of the room), and **Full House** (the whole restaurant, up to
70). Prices are per guest and there is a toggle between **weeknight** and **weekend**
rates. **Chef's Table is the package the restaurant promotes**: it is what roughly two
thirds of bookings land on, it is what the events email quotes first, and on the page it
is meant to be the obvious middle choice — the raised, dark card wearing a single
"Most popular" tag. Exactly one package carries that tag, it is Chef's Table, and it
stays there no matter which rate the visitor is looking at. Hearthside and Full House
stay plain.
