# Brief — The Blue Iris Supper Club (restaurant, landing page)

Build the marketing landing page for **The Blue Iris Supper Club**, a
seventy-four seat Northwoods supper club that has stood at 4118 Cedar Bluff Road
on the east shore of Lake Wanetka, in Harlow Bend, since **Stanley and Vera
Ostrowski opened it in 1946**. Their granddaughter **Delia Ostrowski** runs it
now, with **Curt Hallenbeck** on the broiler (twenty-two years), **Rosa
Villareal** on pastry and the relish tray, **Junior Wexler** behind the brandy
bar, and **Margo Doyle** running the dining room. The whole point of the place is
the order of a supper club evening: you arrive early, you have a pressed-and-sweet
brandy old fashioned at the bar while the room fills, the relish tray and a crock
of braunschweiger land before you have ordered anything, and only then does supper
come — broiled Lake Wanetka walleye, prime rib cut to order off the Saturday
roast, Friday's lake perch fry in a cracker-meal crust, Sunday's Chicken
Ostrowski from Vera's 1951 recipe card, and a grasshopper pie that has not
changed since the Kennedy administration. There is a private room upstairs, **the
Lantern Room**, that seats twenty-two under the original 1946 fixtures. Wednesday
through Sunday, 4:30 onward, closed Monday and Tuesday. Phone is (555) 0179.
Reservations are taken for the dining room and the Lantern Room; the bar is
always walk-in, and a third of the room is held for people who just show up.

The page should read like the place looks: pine-dark and warm, cream paper, brass
and amber light, a little oxblood, deep section rhythm, real inline-SVG artwork
(the neon iris sign over the lake at dusk, the relish tray, a pressed old
fashioned, the Lantern Room) instead of stock photography. Tone: plainspoken,
proud of the routine, faintly funny, never nostalgic for its own sake — copy
written by people who know exactly how many perch go in a basket.

**Sections, in order:** header with nav, phone and a reserve button; a hero with
the room's promise, two calls to action and four facts; a credential bar with
press lines and numbers; the supper menu — six dishes with prices and a sentence
each; **the reservations block, whose "Reserve a table" button opens a modal
dialog over the page — the guest picks a room, sees the hold policy and the phone
number, and can dismiss the dialog by clicking the dimmed page behind it, by
pressing Escape, or with its close button**; the Friday fish fry, in a deep-dive
block; the relish tray, in a second deep-dive block; the brandy bar and how an old
fashioned is built here; how an evening actually runs, in five numbered steps; the
Lantern Room and its three table configurations; three named guest voices plus
press quotes; six front-of-house questions answered; hours, address, driving,
docking and access details; a closing reservation CTA; and a four-column footer.
