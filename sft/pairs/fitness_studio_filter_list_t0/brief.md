# Brief — Tidewater Strength & Movement (fitness_studio · landing)

Build the marketing landing page for **Tidewater Strength & Movement**, a 5,400
square foot coaching gym at 92 Ropewalk Street in the Fell's Landing district of
Baltimore, founded in 2017 by head coach Marisol Reyes after eleven years in
collegiate weight rooms. Tidewater is not a bootcamp and not a big-box gym: it is
a coached room with fourteen lifting platforms, a forty-metre turf lane, six
rowers and a whiteboard where members write their numbers down, and it sells a
promise aimed squarely at people with unreasonable schedules — nurses off a night
shift, cooks who finish at midnight, parents with a ninety-minute window. Every
member starts with a free intro and a movement screen, gets a written plan they
physically keep, and trains in groups capped at twelve so a coach can actually
watch them lift. The voice is plain, warm and unimpressed by fitness marketing:
it names real weights, real times, real prices and real people instead of
adjectives, and it never promises a transformation in thirty days. Contact
details are (410) 555 0176 and hello@tidewaterstrength.com; the room is open
Monday to Friday 5:30am–8:30pm, Saturday 7am–1pm and Sunday 8am–noon. Coaches to
name throughout: Marisol Reyes (head coach and founder), Dez Okafor (strength),
Priya Raghavan (conditioning), Theo Lindqvist (movement and recovery). Members to
quote by name: Alina Petrov, an emergency nurse at Harbor General; Wendell
Boakye, a middle-school principal who came back to lifting at fifty-eight; and
Ruth Kanellis, a sous chef at Marrow & Vine.

Ship a long-form landing page on a bone ground (`#F7F2EA`) with deep harbor-navy
ink (`#0B2A33`), a signal orange (`#E4682A`) and a sea-green accent (`#1D7A6B`),
every visual drawn as inline SVG or a CSS gradient because no network is
available. It needs: a site header with the drawn wave-and-bar mark, five
destinations, the phone number and an intro CTA; a hero carrying the studio name,
a subhead of at least twenty-five words, two calls to action, three headline
numbers and a drawn illustration of the platform room; a trust bar with four hard
numbers and five partner wordmarks; a five-card program grid with real monthly
prices and drawn icons; **the anchor section — this week's class schedule, twelve
named sessions across four tracks (Strength, Conditioning, Mobility,
Semi-private) with a row of category buttons above them and a line that reports
how many of the twelve are on screen**; three alternating deep-dive blocks that
explain the movement screen, the written plan and the numbers wall; a four-coach
roster with drawn monogram portraits and real credentials; a four-step path from
first phone call to first full training block; three member stories with named
attribution and a results row; a facility section with the equipment list, the
opening hours table and what is in the changing rooms; a six-question FAQ
answering what someone nervous about a barbell actually asks; a closing
conversion block built around a short labelled intro-booking form that confirms
in place; and a four-column footer with the address, the hours and the legal row.

The schedule is the working heart of the page. Twelve sessions are listed by
default and the category buttons above them narrow the list to a single track —
choosing **Strength** must leave only the four strength sessions on screen and
take the rowing, mobility and semi-private ones away — while **All classes**
brings the full twelve back, and the summary line above the grid always agrees
with what a visitor can actually count on screen.
