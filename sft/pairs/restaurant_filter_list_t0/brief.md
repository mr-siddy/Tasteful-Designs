# Brief — Persimmon & Rye (restaurant landing page)

Build the marketing landing page for **Persimmon & Rye**, a 54-seat wood-fired
restaurant at 214 Monroe Street in Germantown, Nashville, open since 2019 and run
by chef-owner **Nell Kavanagh** with general manager **Tomás Vela**. Everything
that reaches a table is cooked over a nine-foot white-oak and hickory hearth built
in the room by metalworker Ruben Salcido — there is no gas range in the building —
and the kitchen buys within a hundred miles: vegetables from Hollow Bend Farm in
Leiper's Fork, cornmeal and grits stone-milled at Cane Ridge Mill, butter and
buttermilk from Windrow Creek Creamery, oysters trucked up twice a week by Two
Rivers Oyster Co. The room's personality is warm and unfussy — paper menus
reprinted daily, a bread program run by Ibrahim Sow, pastry by Junie Park, no
dress code, a bar that will pour you something non-alcoholic that took as much
work as the wine. Service is Wednesday to Saturday from 5pm, plus a Sunday supper
served family-style from noon to three. Reservations on (615) 555 0192.

The page must be a full-length landing page, not a teaser: a sticky header with
the brand mark and five destinations plus a *Book a table* action; a hero with the
restaurant's name, a real 25-word subhead, two calls to action and a hearth
illustration; a proof bar of awards, ratings and numbers; the four ways to eat
here (bar seats, hearth counter, dining room, the Loft) with real per-person
prices; **the menu section — fourteen dishes a guest can narrow with a row of
course buttons (All dishes, Small plates, From the hearth, Pasta & grains,
Sweets), with a summary line above the grid that says how many of the fourteen are
showing**; a deep dive on the hearth itself and on the bread and fire in
alternating copy/visual blocks; a growers section naming the farms and what each
one sends; the shape of an evening in numbered steps; three named guest
testimonials; the private dining loft; a six-question FAQ that answers the things
people actually phone about; a closing reservation block; and a four-column footer
with hours, address, contact and legal.

Give it a real identity rather than default grays: persimmon `#C4552B` and moss
`#4A5D3A` on a warm paper `#FBF6EE` ground with near-black `#241C17` ink, heavy
tracking-tight display type against relaxed body copy, generous section padding,
alternating section backgrounds so the page has rhythm as you scroll, and every
image, icon, logo and illustration drawn as inline SVG or a CSS gradient — the
build has no network, so no remote image URLs anywhere. Write the actual copy of a
restaurant that exists: named dishes with prices and a sentence of description
each, named people, named farms, real hours, real phone number. No placeholder
text of any kind.
