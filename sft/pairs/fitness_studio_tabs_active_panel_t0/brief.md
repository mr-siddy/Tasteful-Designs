# Brief — Tidemark Row Club (fitness_studio · landing)

Build the single-page marketing site for **Tidemark Row Club**, a 3,100 sq ft indoor
rowing, strength and recovery studio on the second floor of the old chandlery at
47 Cordage Lane, in the Bellhaven Locks district. It opened in March 2018 and is run
by Ines Okonkwo — nine years on a national lightweight squad before she ever coached —
and Callum Reyes-Barrett, a strength coach who spent six years rehabbing rowers who had
been trained into the ground. The room holds eighteen water-resistance ergs on a sprung
floor, a six-platform strength annexe, and a recovery bay with a cedar sauna and two
contrast plunges. Sessions cap at eighteen people with three coaches on the floor, and
the club's whole argument is that rowing is a *skill* sport that most gyms sell as a
punishment: every member gets their 2k split tested on day one, trains an eight-week
written block, and gets re-tested at the end of it, so progress is a number on a page
and not a feeling. The audience is adults 30–55 — ex-team-sport people, desk workers
with cranky lower backs, and a real contingent of masters rowers off the water in
winter — who want hard training that will not wreck them.

The page has to carry three things at once. First, the **room**: sprung floor, the
water-resistance ergs and why they matter, the strength annexe, the cedar sauna and
plunges, the fact that it smells like cedar and not like a big-box gym. Second, the
**method**: the Landing Session, the split test, the eight-week block, the re-test —
laid out as numbered steps with real detail. Third, the **programme**, which is the
centrepiece: the club runs three training tracks — **Row**, **Strength** and
**Restore** — and a visitor picks between them with a row of tabs to see that track's
sessions, each with a name, a length, a target and a one-line description of what it
actually feels like. Only the chosen track's sessions should be on screen at a time.

Around that: a header with nav and a "Book a landing session" action; a hero with the
Bellhaven Locks address and two calls to action; a proof strip with member count,
rating, erg count and founding year; a weekly timetable; deep-dive blocks on the room
and the equipment; the four coaches with their real backgrounds; four membership tiers
with prices (Day Pass £26, Ebb £119/mo, Flood £169/mo, Crew £249/mo); three named
testimonials with roles and what changed for them; five FAQs answered properly
(soreness, whether you need rowing experience, parking, contracts, the sauna); a
closing call to action; and a four-column footer with nav, contact, hours and legal.

Visual direction: harbour-at-dawn, not neon gym. Deep sea-ink and teal grounds
(#062b30, #0b3a41, #12626c) against a warm sand-cream page (#faf6ef, #f1e7d7), with a
single copper accent (#e0813d) used only for actions and highlights. Tight, confident
display headings with wide-tracked uppercase eyebrows above them; generous section
padding; rounded cards with soft shadows; alternating section backgrounds so the page
has rhythm as you scroll. No stock photography — every visual is inline SVG (a rope-knot
brand mark, a stroke-arc erg illustration, a section-wide water-line divider, an icon
per feature) plus CSS gradients for the hero and closing grounds.
