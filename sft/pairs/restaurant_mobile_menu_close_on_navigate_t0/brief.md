# Brief — Copperfern (restaurant, landing page)

Build the marketing landing page for **Copperfern**, a 62-seat wood-fire
restaurant at 2140 NW Market Street in Ballard, Seattle, two blocks off the
ship canal where the fishing fleet ties up. Everything on the menu meets live
flame at some point: a single seven-foot hearth burns Washington apple and
alder from three in the afternoon, and the kitchen cooks off its three heats —
open flame, glowing coals, and the cooling embers where things rest overnight.
Chef-owner Nadia Kowalczyk opened the room in 2019 after nine years cooking in
Basque country; grill cook Tomás Iriarte runs the fire; June Sasaki bakes the
hearth bread and the buckwheat honey tart. The room is warm and unfussy —
reclaimed fir, copper sconces, a twelve-seat chef's counter facing the coals,
and a loft upstairs that seats twenty-eight for private dinners. Dinner runs
Wednesday through Sunday, 5–10pm, with a Sunday hearth roast at midday.
Reservations open thirty days out; about a quarter of the room is held for
walk-ins at the bar.

The page must read like a real restaurant's site, not a template: a sticky
header with a copper fern brand mark, five destinations (Menus, The Fire,
Sourcing, Private Events, Visit) and a reserve button; a hero that names the
place and the cooking; a press-and-numbers strip; six dishes with real prices
and a sentence each; a deep-dive explaining the three heats of the hearth; a
sourcing section naming the boats and farms the kitchen buys from; a walk
through how an evening actually runs; the loft and chef's-counter private
options; three named guest quotes; five honest FAQ answers about parking,
dietary needs, kids, corkage and the walk-in bar; a closing reservation block
with a working request form; and a four-column footer with hours, address and
contact. Palette: deep forest ink (#12261F), ember copper (#C4653A / #E08B4F),
warm cream (#F7F1E8) and a moss mid-tone, with a serif display face against a
clean sans body, generous section padding and a consistent 2xl radius. No
network is available, so every visual is inline SVG or a CSS gradient — the
fern mark, the press wordmarks, the hearth illustration, the sourcing map, the
step icons.

**The interaction this page is anchored on:** on a phone the header collapses
to a hamburger toggle that opens a full-width menu panel over the page. Tapping
any destination in that panel — or the reserve button inside it — must send the
guest to that section *and* close the panel behind them, so the page they asked
for is what they actually see.
