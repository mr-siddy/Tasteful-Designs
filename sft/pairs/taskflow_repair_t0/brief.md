# Brief — Taskflow marketing site (SaaS · landing)

Build the marketing landing page for **Taskflow**, a dispatch and job-scheduling
platform for small field-service contractors — HVAC, plumbing, electrical, solar
and roofing shops running between five and eighty technicians. Taskflow is based
in Lawrenceville, Pittsburgh, and was started by two people who used to run the
phones at a forty-truck heating and cooling company, so the whole product is
organised around one screen: a live dispatch board where every job, van and
technician sits in one place and a change to any one of them rebuilds the rest of
the day. The voice is plain-spoken and operational, never breathless — it sells
to an owner and a dispatch lead who measure their week in first-time fix rate,
drive time and overtime, not in "synergy". Prices are per technician per month:
Crew at $39, Shop at $59, Fleet at $89, billed annually, no per-job fees.

The page should scroll like a real product site: a sticky header with the
wordmark, five destinations and a demo button; a hero that states the promise and
shows the board itself; a proof bar of named contractor customers and hard
numbers; six platform capabilities; a three-block deep dive on the board, on
rerouting a forty-stop day, and on paperwork; a four-step walkthrough of a job
from the first phone call to the invoice going out that night; the tools it
connects to; three pricing tiers; three testimonials from named dispatchers and
owners at those customers; six real FAQs about migration, offline work,
contracts and support; a closing demo block; and a four-column footer with
address, hours and legal. Commit to a visual identity — ink-slate backgrounds,
an amber "in motion" accent, a cyan secondary, generous section rhythm — and draw
every logo, icon, illustration and background flourish as inline SVG or a CSS
gradient, because the page must render with no network at all.

Because dispatchers live on their phones, the header carries a hamburger toggle
that opens a full-screen navigation sheet on small viewports: an overlay, a close
control, the five destinations with a line of description each, a "Book a demo"
call to action and the support phone number. Choosing any destination in that
sheet must take the visitor to it *and* put the sheet away — a navigation sheet
that stays open after you have chosen where to go is a sheet covering the page
you asked for.
