# Brief — Foxglove Dental Studio (dental_clinic, landing)

Build the single-page marketing site for **Foxglove Dental Studio**, a twelve-year-old
family and restorative dental practice at 1140 Ellery Avenue in the Cobblehill Ward of
Wren Harbor. It was opened in 2013 by **Dr. Priya Raghunathan, DDS** after nine years in
a high-volume corporate group, and the whole practice is a reaction to that: eight
appointments a day instead of twenty-two, forty-five minute hygiene visits, one
treatment plan written in plain language with the price on it before anything is
scheduled. Roughly a third of the patient list arrived describing themselves as afraid
of the dentist, so nitrous, numbing gel before the needle, weighted blankets and a
stop-anytime hand signal are standard rather than an upgrade. The voice of the page
should match the room — calm, unhurried, specific about money, never pushy, never
cutesy about teeth. Palette: deep spruce (#0F3B36) with a lighter forest mid-tone, a
dusty foxglove rose (#C2557E) as the single accent, and a warm linen neutral ramp
(#FBF8F3 → #F3EDE4) instead of clinical white; serif display headings with real weight
contrast against a clean sans body; generous section padding, soft 2xl radii, low warm
shadows. Alternate section backgrounds (linen → spruce → cream) so the page has rhythm
as a visitor scrolls. Every visual is inline SVG or a CSS gradient — a foxglove-stem
brand mark, an arched hero illustration of the operatory window, an icon set for
services and process steps, a same-day-crown mill diagram, a sedation comfort scale, a
digital-scan mesh, geometric portrait avatars for the team, and mesh/gradient washes
behind the hero and closing CTA. No network is available, so no remote images.

The page runs long and flat: a sticky header with six destinations, the phone number
and a book button; a hero with a real subhead; a trust bar of four practice numbers and
four credentials; six services with honest from-prices; **the membership plan section**;
three alternating deep-dive blocks (same-day crowns, sedation and anxiety care, digital
scanning instead of trays); a five-step first-visit walkthrough; the four people who run
the practice; three named patient testimonials; six real FAQs about insurance, waiting
times, children and emergencies; a visit block with hours, address, accepted insurance
and a reminder-list form; a closing CTA; and a four-column footer.

The section the whole page is anchored on is the **in-house membership plan**, which is
how Foxglove serves the roughly forty percent of its patients who carry no dental
insurance. There are three memberships — **Bright Start** ($32/month, two cleanings and
two exams a year for a healthy adult), **Complete Care** ($54/month, three cleanings, a
same-day emergency slot, night-guard fitting, whitening trays and 20% off restorative
work), and **Whole Family** ($118/month, everything in Complete Care for up to four
people in one household). Prices are shown per month, with a toggle to a
pay-in-full-yearly rate that saves two months. **Complete Care is the membership the
practice promotes**: it is the one Dr. Raghunathan recommends at the end of a first
visit, it is where about two thirds of new members land, and on the page it is meant to
be the obvious middle choice — the raised, spruce-dark card wearing a single
"Most popular" tag. Exactly one plan carries that tag, it is Complete Care, and it stays
there whichever billing cadence the visitor is looking at. Bright Start and Whole Family
stay plain.
