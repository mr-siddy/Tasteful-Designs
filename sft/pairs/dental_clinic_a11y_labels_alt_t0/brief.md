# Brief — Cedar & Vale Dental Studio (landing page)

Build the marketing landing page for **Cedar & Vale Dental Studio**, a
fourteen-chair family dental practice at 14 Sorrel Street in the Ashbourne Green
neighbourhood, led by principal dentist Dr. Priya Nandakumar. Their entire
positioning is *hours that fit the week you actually have*: chairs open at seven
in the morning and the last one goes down at eight at night Monday to Thursday,
Saturdays run eight to four, every registered patient is promised an emergency
slot inside the same week, there is a soundproofed quiet room for people who
dread the chair, and a separate children's floor with its own waiting area so a
parent can bring two kids at once. The page has to convert three people: someone
who has skipped check-ups for years because they cannot take a weekday morning
off, a parent booking a first visit for a five-year-old, and a nervous adult who
needs to be told exactly what will happen before they will pick up the phone.

Ship a full long-form page: a header with the cedar-and-tooth mark, five
destinations, the phone number and a booking call to action; a hero that leads on
the seven-to-eight opening hours with an inline SVG illustration and two calls to
action; a proof strip carrying the 4.9 rating, the review count, years open,
registered patients and the insurers billed directly; a six-card service grid with
from-prices and appointment lengths; **the booking panel** — a two-column block
with a row of round quick-contact controls, three photographs of the studio, and
the appointment request form; three alternating deep-dive blocks on the quiet
room, the digital scanner that replaced impression trays, and the children's
floor; a five-step walkthrough of a first visit; the four-person team with
photographs, names, roles and one-line biographies; a weekly hours table with
parking, tram and step-free access notes and what to bring; three membership
plans for patients without insurance; three named patient testimonials; six real
FAQs about cost, nerves, children, emergencies, aligners and getting there; a
closing call to action; and a four-column footer.

The booking panel is the heart of the page. Its quick-contact rail is four round
icon-only controls — call the front desk, send a text, get directions, email the
front desk — and pressing one opens a small detail card underneath with the
number, the SMS line, the address or the email address. Beside the rail sit three
photographs: the quiet room, a treatment room and the front desk. The request form
asks for a full name, an email address, a mobile number, a preferred day, a reason
for visiting and an optional note, plus a reminder opt-in, and submitting it swaps
the form for a confirmation that greets the patient by first name and repeats the
day they asked for.

Everything on the page must be announceable by a screen reader: every photograph
carries alt text that says what it shows, every icon-only control says what it
does, and every box in the request form is tied to the wording printed beside it,
so landing in a box announces the right field.

Visual direction: deep harbour blue and a mint accent over a cool porcelain
neutral ramp, large rounded cards, soft shadows, generous section padding, and
alternating section backgrounds for rhythm. All artwork is inline SVG, CSS
gradients or repo-local SVG files — no network images. Copy is warm, concrete and
free of clinical jargon; every claim names a number, a time or a person.
