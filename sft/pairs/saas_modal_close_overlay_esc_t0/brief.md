# Brief — Portway marketing site (SaaS landing page)

Build the marketing landing page for **Portway**, a customs-documentation
platform sold to mid-size ocean freight forwarders and 3PLs — the kind of shop
that moves 400 to 9,000 containers a year out of Tacoma, Savannah and Long Beach
and still keeps its ISF deadlines on a whiteboard. Portway was built inside one
of those forwarders by Dana Ruiz and Peter Ngo after a $12,400 late-ISF penalty,
and the whole product is aimed at one promise: the paperwork clears before the
ship does. It ingests the commercial invoice and packing list off email, reads
the line items, proposes HTS classifications with the ruling it leaned on,
watches the CBP 7501 and ISF-10 clocks against the vessel's ETD, and puts every
missing document on one exception desk instead of in six inboxes. Voice: exact,
unhurried, operator-to-operator — real filing names, real deadlines in hours,
real dollar figures, no growth-hack breathlessness and no "revolutionize."

Ship a long-form page in a harbour palette (navy `#0B1F33`, dock teal `#0D6E8C`,
signal amber `#F2A03D`, warm slate neutrals) with real inline-SVG artwork: a
brand mark, customer wordmarks, a hero dashboard showing filing clocks, a
shipment timeline, a document-engine diagram, integration tiles and quote
portraits. No network images.

Sections, top to bottom: sticky header with five destinations and a "Book a
walkthrough" CTA; hero with the ETD-clock illustration and two CTAs; a trust bar
with named forwarders and hard numbers; the filing workbench (six capabilities);
**a walkthrough-booking section whose button opens a modal dialog** where the
visitor picks their annual container volume and sends the request — the dialog
must dismiss on a click of the dimmed backdrop, on Escape, and on its own ×, and
must stay open while the visitor is using it; a shipment-timeline deep dive; the
classification/document engine deep dive; the exception desk; the integration
grid; a four-step rollout plan; three pricing tiers with real monthly prices;
three testimonials from named operations people at named companies; a security
and recordkeeping block; a six-question FAQ answered honestly; a closing CTA; and
a four-column footer with product, company, resources and contact details for the
Tacoma office.
